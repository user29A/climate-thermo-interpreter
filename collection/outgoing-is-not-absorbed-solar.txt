Outgoing planetary power S(1-alpha)/4 is not absorbed sunlight

The figure 240 W/m2, the equivalent temperature 255 K, and the equivalent -18 C are Earth's outgoing planetary emission, or the blackbody equivalent of that emission. The expression S(1-alpha)/4 is the outgoing. It is not the incoming. It is not the absorbed. It must not be called a spherical average of absorbed sunlight, even as a pedagogical device.

The solar constant S is about 1370 W/m2. That is the flux of the solar beam on a surface facing the Sun at Earth's distance. On the sunlit face the local solar input is S cos(z) times the local co-albedo at each point, with z the zenith angle. At the subsolar point the zenith angle is zero and the flux is of order S. At the terminator it is zero. On the night hemisphere it is zero. A time average of solar input is therefore an average over a hemisphere, and it always retains a zenith maximum. That geometry is not a uniform full-sphere flux of 240 W/m2.

Dividing the intercepted solar power by the area of the sphere produces the arithmetic S/4, and multiplying by (1-alpha) produces S(1-alpha)/4. That division spreads a dayside beam over the night side and over latitudes that never received it. The result is not a physical solar flux at any location, and it is not an absorbed-solar field that can then be partitioned between atmosphere and ground. When the same numerical value, about 240 W/m2, is measured or inferred as planetary outgoing longwave, that value is the output. Naming the output with the symbol S(1-alpha)/4 does not convert the output into an input. It simply has neither geometric nor physical meaning to use that value as solar input.

This identification matters because S(1-alpha)/4, if used as an input when it is truly only the diluted output, implies that the Sun cannot create the climate and temperature which we actually experience from it. Treating S(1-alpha)/4 as the solar input leaves a massive gap in the natural solar explanation for the climate and for the known ground and atmospheric responses to sunlight. It makes it seem as if the Sun cannot place maximum initial heating at the ground surface in the first place.

Energy is what is conserved, not flux. Energy input and energy output are equal. Flux input and flux output are not, because energy in and energy out occur over different total surface areas. The incoming solar beam is a flux on the illuminated disk and on the day hemisphere. The outgoing planetary emission is a flux from the radiating Earth, day and night. Those are different surfaces and different time structures. The two fluxes need not be the same number, and they cannot be: a hemispheric beam with a zenith maximum cannot be identical to a whole-sphere outgoing emission. Equating them numerically, and then calling 240 W/m2 the spherical average of absorbed sunlight equal to planetary outgoing longwave, is the identification that invents a 255 K no-atmosphere planet and a 33 K greenhouse increment to +15 C. That identification is false.

A portion of the actual solar beam is absorbed in the atmosphere, chiefly by water vapour, ozone, and clouds, and thermalizes there. That is ordinary absorption of energy from a hotter source, the Sun. It heats the air that absorbs it. It supplies no 240 W/m2 solar term to be split at the ground, and it supplies no mechanism by which cooler air can heat a warmer surface. Heat is the one-way action of the hotter body on the colder body.

The ground's energy budget is not the planetary outgoing budget. The surface obeys a time-dependent First Law. Heat performed on the ground exists only from a hotter source; in general that is the sunlight that actually reaches the ground, which varies in space and time and is zero at night. Substituting S(1-alpha)/4 or 240 W/m2 into the surface budget replaces the local solar beam with the planet's output and is a change of problem.

The -18 C figure is the blackbody equivalent of Earth's outgoing planetary power S(1-alpha)/4, and it is the observed temperature near the mass-weighted mid-level of the troposphere, about 5 km. It is not a surface temperature the ground would have in the absence of greenhouse gases, and it is not obtained by treating 240 W/m2 as absorbed sunlight. From the First Law and hydrostatic balance the dry adiabat is dT/dh = -g/C_p approximately -9.8 K/km. Latent heat reduces this to the environmental mean near -6.5 K/km. An average of a linear profile cannot sit at an extremity; the mass-weighted mid-level is near H ln 2 approximately 5.8 km (H approximately 8.4 km), and the observed -18 C is near 5 km. Surface temperature is that average plus the integrated lapse: about -18 C + 6.5 K/km times 5 km approximately +15 C. Atmospheric absorption of sunlight from the Sun does not reopen a radiative greenhouse. A rival complete cause of the same numbers must compute them with explicit equations and a residual this accounting does not already fix.
